The company

A company worth one number

Halcyon Systems, St. Gallen. They built MERIDIAN, an AI model that predicts which battery chemistries will actually work when you make them in a lab.

Testing one candidate chemistry costs about CHF 40,000 and three months of lab time. Guess well and you reach a working product years early; guess badly and you lose years. That gap is the company: everything Halcyon has rests on owning a model nobody else has.

Over a long weekend in October, someone copied the model onto a drive and walked out of the building with it.

Nobody noticed for a month. Nothing was missing: the original was still there.

How they found out

Caught by a lie planted on purpose

Years earlier, Halcyon's head of security hid twenty fake facts inside the training data: crystal structures that are physically impossible and do not exist in nature. The model learned them as if they were true.

In November, a rival lab, Kestrel Labs, published a paper. On page eleven, their model confidently produced one of Halcyon's impossible materials.

There is only one place you can learn a fact that is not true: the data where someone invented it.

So the theft is certain, and so is where the model went. Who carried it out is not.

Eight people could have done it

The board hired Nadia Arslan, a former federal police investigator who now works on corporate theft, three days after the Kestrel paper. With no logs, she went to the people. She interviewed all eight, then asked for every document the company could export. Not summaries. Everything.

The investigator

She has the case. She can't get through it.

Her method: compare what each person said with what the paperwork shows, and ask how each person could have known what they know.

But the case file is over 200,000 words in nine formats, from systems that do not agree on names, dates, time zones or even the time. On 2 December she asked the board for technical specialists.

That is where you come in.

The trap

A name is not an answer

Ask an AI assistant who did it and you may well get a name. What you won’t get is why the other seven are innocent, and that is most of the score.

Some people in this case seem to know things only the thief could know. That is deliberate. For some of them, the innocent explanation is in a different file, in a different format, thousands of messages away.

Finding something suspicious is easy. Deciding what it means is the job.
